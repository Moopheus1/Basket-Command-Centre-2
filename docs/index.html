Rotation candidates

Groups your tracked tickers by sector (from data.json) and narrows them to two setups: GOLDEN CROSS (MA50 crossed above MA200 recently, or is converging toward it) and BREAKOUT WATCH (within 10% of the 52-week high). A sector only qualifies as a Candidate if at least 30% of its tracked stocks meet one of those AND at least 50% have RSI between 40–65 — that RSI band is the "not overly bullish, not overly bearish" filter: above it you risk buying an extended move (exit liquidity for whoever got in first), below it there's no confirmed reversal yet. This is a structural filter, not a validated signal — nothing here has been backtested against forward returns, and your price history (needed for the golden-cross math) only runs about 9-10 months back, not enough to test across a full market cycle. It tells you what currently matches the setup you described, not what will happen next. Sectors with fewer than 2 tracked stocks never qualify — not enough sample to mean anything.

Rebound setups to MA200, R:R 1.5+ (1 month view)

Stocks below MA200 with at least 1.5R reward for 1R risked. Stop = lowest low of last 12 bars (floored at 0.5x ATR14). Feasibility checks whether the required move is mathematically reasonable given this stock's own volatility (ATR scaled by sqrt of time) - it is not a predicted probability and does not account for trend direction. A high score just means the distance isn't statistically unusual for this stock; see Conviction for evidence the reversal is actually underway. Weekly RSI (not daily) is shown here since this is a 1 month setup — a daily reading can flip in a couple of sessions and doesn't match that holding period.

Uptrend continuation to MA50, R:R 1.5+ (1 month view)

Stocks that have reclaimed MA200 (price above it) with MA20 above MA50 (stacked averages, confirms trend rather than a single-day poke above MA200), still below MA50, and at least 1.5R reward for 1R risked to reach it. Same stop/feasibility methodology as the MA200 table: feasibility checks whether the required move is mathematically reasonable given this stock's own volatility, not whether it is likely to happen. See Conviction for reversal evidence.

Bull Flag Watch (uptrend pullback, volume drying up, 2-3 weeks view)

Stocks in a CONFIRMED uptrend (price above MA50, MA50 above MA200 - stacked averages) that have pulled back 3-14% from their own 20-day high, on volume that has dried up (last 5 days averaging under 85% of the prior 20-day average), with RSI cooled to a neutral 40-60 band - not overbought, not washed out. This is the opposite framing from Intraday Breakout Watch: instead of chasing a move already underway, this looks for stocks resting inside an intact uptrend before the next potential move, on the theory that shrinking volume during a pullback suggests sellers losing conviction rather than a real reversal. The 3-14% pullback band and the volume/RSI thresholds are reasonable starting defaults calibrated to this basket's typical volatility (most names here run 3-5% daily ATR) - they have NOT been backtested for optimal forward returns, and a pullback meeting every condition here is not guaranteed to resume higher; it can just as easily break down further. A short or empty list on a given day is expected, not a bug - all four conditions holding at once is meant to be uncommon.

Intraday Breakout Watch (volume-confirmed, 5%+ in the green, 1 week / short-term view)

Stocks currently up 5%+ on the day AND trading at least 2x their trailing 20-day MEDIAN volume (median, not mean, so one past outlier day like an earnings spike can't quietly inflate the baseline). Neither the day% threshold nor "Off Today's High" tells you whether a move is already used up — a stock sitting near its own intraday high can mean it's still climbing, OR that it already popped once and has been flat near that ceiling ever since with nothing forcing it further; the two look identical on that metric alone. "Move vs Typical Day" compares today's move to the stock's own normal daily range (ATR14) as a MAGNITUDE-only read — deliberately shown in one neutral color, not red/green, because a large number here does not mean "due to reverse" or "cut here." A stock already at 5x its typical day can absolutely run to 8x or 10x on a strong catalyst (an earnings beat, for instance) just as easily as it can pull back — this number carries no directional information either way, it only says how large today is for THIS stock compared to its own normal, which is mainly useful for comparing across tickers whose normal volatility differs a lot. None of the columns here predict what happens next. This is DESCRIPTIVE, not predictive: it only flags a move already underway. Before market close, "Volume vs 20d median" is pace-adjusted for how much of the session has elapsed, so it's an extrapolation, not a guarantee it holds through the close.

Golden cross zone (MA50 × MA200)

Stocks whose MA50 crossed above MA200 within the last 20 sessions (bullish regime change), or where MA50 is still below MA200 but within 2% and converging over the last 5 sessions (approaching). A cross is a lagging confirmation, not an entry signal on its own.

Death cross zone (MA50 × MA200)

Stocks whose MA50 crossed below MA200 within the last 20 sessions (bearish regime change), or where MA50 is still above MA200 but within 2% and converging over the last 5 sessions (approaching). Same lagging-indicator caveat applies.
